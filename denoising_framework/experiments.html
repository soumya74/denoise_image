Network Debugging & Optimization Report

Architecture: Disentangled Dual-Decoder Denoising Network
Objective: Isolate noise into a 4-D embedding space for deterministic inference subtraction (y - n_hat).

Phase 1: The Scale Disparity & The Illusion of Small Numbers

Diagnosis

Initial training showed the main reconstruction losses converging smoothly, but the Latent Content Consistency Loss was actively increasing.

  • Gradient Swamping: The pixel-level losses operated in the 0.10 to 0.40 range, while the latent loss operated in the 10⁻⁴ range. The Adam optimizer ignored the latent alignment because it was mathematically insignificant compared to the image reconstruction errors.
  • Unbounded Feature Growth: Without normalization in the encoder, the raw numerical values of the latent embeddings inflated epoch-over-epoch to achieve better contrast, causing the absolute L1 distance to rise even if structural alignment was improving.

The Fix

Introduced a Cross-Reconstruction Consistency Loss. By penalizing the difference between Clean Decoder outputs in the normalized RGB space, we translated the latent mismatch into a pixel-scale penalty that the optimizer could not ignore.

Phase 2: The "Lazy" Decoder & Teacher-Student Dynamics

Diagnosis

Despite increasing the loss weights for the latent and cross-consistency constraints (to 1.6x), both metrics continued to rise.

  • The Shock Absorber Effect: The Clean Decoder was too deep and powerful. It learned to ignore the noise leaking into the 28-D embedding and output a perfect clean image anyway. This "lazy" shortcut bypassed the goal of strict disentanglement in the encoder.
  • Moving Targets: By pulling both embeddings toward each other, the noisy path was actively corrupting the clean path's embedding.

The Fix

  1. Stop-Gradient (.detach()): Modified the loss functions to freeze the clean path during backward propagation. This turned the clean embedding into a strict "Teacher" and forced the noisy embedding ("Student") to match it.
  2. Architectural Crippling: Severely reduced the capacity of the Clean Decoder (down to 3 shallow layers ending in a Sigmoid) so it could no longer hide the encoder's mistakes.
  3. Inference Prioritization: Deepened the Noise Decoder to 64 channels and massively shifted loss weights to prioritize the 4-D noise extraction needed for inference.

Phase 3: Catastrophic Gradient Explosion & Mode Collapse

Diagnosis

The network completely collapsed. The Latent Content difference exploded past 600, Clean Cross dropped to exactly 0.000, and PSNR flatlined at ~5 dB.

  • The Cause: We applied "hard constraints" (architectural bounds) and "soft constraints" (15x loss multipliers) simultaneously. This caused the gradients to explode.
  • Mode Collapse: The exploded latent values hit the new Sigmoid layer in the shallow Clean Decoder, instantly saturating the outputs to solid white/black squares. Because the outputs were identical blank squares, the Cross Loss dropped to zero, and the PSNR crashed.

The Fix

  1. Weight Reset: Reverted the massively inflated loss weights back to a stable 1.0–2.0 range, relying entirely on the architectural .detach() constraints to enforce alignment.
  2. Gradient Clipping: Added an "explosion shield" to the training loop: torch.nn.utils.clip_grad_norm_.
  3. Latent Bounding (The Final Key): To prevent unbounded feature growth without using statistically unstable Batch Normalization, we wrapped the Encoder's output heads in nn.Tanh(). This strictly locked all latent values between [-1.0, 1.0].

Phase 4: Stable Pareto Optimal Front & Inference Readiness

Diagnosis

The architecture and training pipeline reached a stable mathematical equilibrium.

  • Latent Convergence: Thanks to .detach() and Tanh(), the Latent Content loss spiked initially and then steadily drove downwards, proving the noisy embedding was successfully cloning the clean embedding.
  • Noise Prioritization: The Noise Reconstruction loss settled at a highly accurate minimum (~0.015), validating that the 4-D noise head is primed for deterministic inference subtraction.
  • The Clean Cross Illusion: Clean Cross initially rose from zero. Early on, the decoder outputs "gray sludge" (zero variance). As it learns to draw sharp edges, it becomes hyper-sensitive to sub-pixel latent variances. The curve safely plateaued at an L1 difference of 0.002 (less than 1/255 pixel values), indicating imperceptible output variance while maintaining an 18 dB PSNR.

Next Steps (Optimization)

To refine the current checkpoint from "stable" to "highly optimized":

  1. Cure "Minimum Bouncing": Increase the batch size (from 16 to 32 or 64) to smooth out the gradient spikes seen in the final 15 epochs.
  2. Capture Trajectory Momentum: Extend training from 40 epochs to 150 epochs.
  3. Learning Rate Scheduler: Implement StepLR (e.g., halving the learning rate every 50 epochs) to allow the optimizer to settle into the absolute lowest loss valleys.
